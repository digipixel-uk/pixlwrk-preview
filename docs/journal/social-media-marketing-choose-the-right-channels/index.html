Social media marketing: choose the right channels instead of trying to be everywhere

A business does not need to post on every social network. Decide what role social should play, where the audience pays attention and what content the team can sustain well.

“We need to do more social media” is not yet a strategy. The useful question is what social media is expected to do for the business.

For one organisation it may be a credibility layer prospects check before making contact. For another it may be a direct acquisition channel. It can also support recruitment, customer education, community, retention or distribution of broader content.

Define the job before choosing the platform

A channel should earn its place in the plan. Start with the outcome: awareness, authority, engagement, leads, sales, support or recruitment. Then look at where the relevant audience already spends attention and how they behave there.

That is more useful than choosing a platform because competitors are using it.

Understand the difference between organic and paid social

Organic social is useful for repeated visibility, proof, personality and relationships with an audience you can reach naturally. Paid social can put a defined message in front of a much larger or more specific audience, but it needs strong creative, targeting, measurement and a good destination after the click.

The two can support each other, but they are not interchangeable.

Choose a content system the business can sustain

A plan that requires five original posts every day is not a good plan if the team can only produce one thoughtful piece each week.

Build around repeatable formats. A useful article might become a short video, a carousel, two opinion posts, an email and several answers to common questions. One piece of thinking can travel through several channels without becoming identical copy everywhere.

Useful recurring content types

  • Questions customers repeatedly ask
  • Short explanations of difficult decisions
  • Examples of work and the problem behind it
  • Evidence, data and lessons from real projects
  • Useful commentary on changes affecting the audience
  • People, process and behind-the-scenes content where it genuinely adds trust

Match the creative to the channel

Repurposing is useful, but simply posting the same image and caption everywhere ignores how different platforms work. The core idea can remain consistent while the format changes.

A LinkedIn post may carry more argument and context. Instagram may need a stronger visual sequence. Short-form video needs an immediate hook. Paid campaigns need creative variations designed for testing rather than a single “perfect” asset.

Give people somewhere useful to go

If social is intended to produce enquiries or sales, the destination matters. Sending every campaign to the home page adds unnecessary work for the visitor.

Use service pages, campaign landing pages, booking flows or relevant content that continues the exact subject that earned the click.

Measure the role of social properly

Not every useful social interaction produces an immediate last-click conversion. Review direct outcomes where they exist, but also consider assisted journeys, branded search, repeat visits, audience growth and the quality of conversations created.

Fewer channels, used deliberately, can be stronger

A focused social presence that reflects the audience, the business and the available creative capacity is usually more useful than being inconsistently present everywhere.

Choose the role, choose the channel, build a sustainable content system and connect attention to a sensible next step.
