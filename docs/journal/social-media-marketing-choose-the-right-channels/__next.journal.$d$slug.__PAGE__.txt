2c:Tf9a,<p class="article-opening">“We need to do more social media” is not yet a strategy. The useful question is what social media is expected to do for the business.</p>
<p>For one organisation it may be a credibility layer prospects check before making contact. For another it may be a direct acquisition channel. It can also support recruitment, customer education, community, retention or distribution of broader content.</p>
<h2 id="define-the-job-before-choosing-the-platform">Define the job before choosing the platform</h2>
<p>A channel should earn its place in the plan. Start with the outcome: awareness, authority, engagement, leads, sales, support or recruitment. Then look at where the relevant audience already spends attention and how they behave there.</p>
<p>That is more useful than choosing a platform because competitors are using it.</p>
<h2 id="understand-the-difference-between-organic-and-paid-social">Understand the difference between organic and paid social</h2>
<p>Organic social is useful for repeated visibility, proof, personality and relationships with an audience you can reach naturally. Paid social can put a defined message in front of a much larger or more specific audience, but it needs strong creative, targeting, measurement and a good destination after the click.</p>
<p>The two can support each other, but they are not interchangeable.</p>
<h2 id="choose-a-content-system-the-business-can-sustain">Choose a content system the business can sustain</h2>
<p>A plan that requires five original posts every day is not a good plan if the team can only produce one thoughtful piece each week.</p>
<p>Build around repeatable formats. A useful article might become a short video, a carousel, two opinion posts, an email and several answers to common questions. One piece of thinking can travel through several channels without becoming identical copy everywhere.</p>
<h3 id="useful-recurring-content-types">Useful recurring content types</h3>
<ul><li>Questions customers repeatedly ask</li><li>Short explanations of difficult decisions</li><li>Examples of work and the problem behind it</li><li>Evidence, data and lessons from real projects</li><li>Useful commentary on changes affecting the audience</li><li>People, process and behind-the-scenes content where it genuinely adds trust</li></ul>
<h2 id="match-the-creative-to-the-channel">Match the creative to the channel</h2>
<p>Repurposing is useful, but simply posting the same image and caption everywhere ignores how different platforms work. The core idea can remain consistent while the format changes.</p>
<p>A LinkedIn post may carry more argument and context. Instagram may need a stronger visual sequence. Short-form video needs an immediate hook. Paid campaigns need creative variations designed for testing rather than a single “perfect” asset.</p>
<h2 id="give-people-somewhere-useful-to-go">Give people somewhere useful to go</h2>
<p>If social is intended to produce enquiries or sales, the destination matters. Sending every campaign to the home page adds unnecessary work for the visitor.</p>
<p>Use service pages, campaign landing pages, booking flows or relevant content that continues the exact subject that earned the click.</p>
<h2 id="measure-the-role-of-social-properly">Measure the role of social properly</h2>
<p>Not every useful social interaction produces an immediate last-click conversion. Review direct outcomes where they exist, but also consider assisted journeys, branded search, repeat visits, audience growth and the quality of conversations created.</p>
<h2 id="fewer-channels-used-deliberately-can-be-stronger">Fewer channels, used deliberately, can be stronger</h2>
<p>A focused social presence that reflects the audience, the business and the available creative capacity is usually more useful than being inconsistently present everywhere.</p>
